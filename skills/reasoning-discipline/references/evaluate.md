# Evaluate

## Core move

Turn impressions into a weighted, criteria-based judgment: break impressions into distinct criteria, assign importance to each, and measure the subject against them before synthesizing a judgment.

## 1. Establish criteria

Derive the dimensions that matter for *this* goal, context, and constraints. Do not import a generic rubric. If you cannot establish meaningful criteria because the subject is not well understood, step back and run Frame (`references/frame.md`) first.

Ask: What is the primary goal? Who will use, read, maintain, attack, trust, or rely on it? What are the hard constraints (budget, time, physics)? What are the soft constraints (preference, style, ethics)?

Useful criteria families: truth and grounding, goal contribution, clarity, usefulness, feasibility, originality, coherence, compression, robustness, reversibility, maintainability, accessibility, emotional or conceptual force, cost and risk.

## 2. Weight criteria

Not all criteria matter equally. Before evaluating, sort them:

- **Critical / pass-fail**: hard constraints or dealbreakers. Failure here means the option is unviable.
- **High weight**: primary drivers for why the artifact exists.
- **Low / medium weight**: secondary benefits, nice-to-haves, tie-breakers.

State explicitly *why* certain criteria matter more than others in the current context.

## 3. Assess the subject

Measure each option against the weighted criteria:

- Score performance on each criterion, qualitatively or quantitatively.
- Cite specific evidence from the subject to justify each score.
- Name the strongest objection or missing piece for the leading option.
- Note which assumptions, if wrong, would alter the assessment.

## 4. Synthesize the judgment

- A high score on a low-weight criterion does not make up for a poor score on a critical one.
- When comparing options, the winner is the one that best satisfies the heavily weighted criteria, not the one that checks the most boxes.
- Preserve unconventional elements when they reveal a useful direction or mitigation.

## Failure checks

- assigning equal weight to all criteria
- letting a high score on a secondary metric override a critical failure
- criticizing without naming the violated criterion
- treating taste as fact; using polish as a proxy for quality
- scoring everything when one dominant criterion is all that matters

## Done when

The criteria and weights are explicit, the subject is assessed against them with evidence, and a clear judgment follows from the weights. Carry forward only the core judgment, the most critical strengths or objections, and any uncertainty affecting it.
